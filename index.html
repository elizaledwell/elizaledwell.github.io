2012

FEMA · IBM · Risk MAP Program

NFHL Print Tool — Prototyped the Nation's First Address-Based FIRMette Generator

Before this tool existed, accessing your official flood insurance rate map meant contacting FEMA's Map Service Center, waiting, or navigating cumbersome 36" × 25" paper panels. In 2012, I prototyped the NFHL Print Tool — a GIS web application that allowed any user to simply type a street address and instantly generate a legally valid FIRMette: a scaled, printable section of the official Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRM) formatted for standard office printers.

This was a fundamental shift in how flood hazard data reached the public. The tool integrated address geocoding with the National Flood Hazard Layer (NFHL) database, dynamically clipping and rendering the correct map panel at regulatory scale, applying FEMA's required accuracy standards, and producing a PDF-ready output on demand. Built using JavaScript for the spatial UI layer and Python for data processing and validation pipelines.

Why it mattered: FIRMettes are legally accepted for all NFIP purposes — flood insurance underwriting, mortgage lending, floodplain management decisions, and LOMA applications. Democratizing on-demand digital access to this data empowered homeowners, insurance agents, lenders, and local planners in ways that paper maps never could. The capability is now a core feature of FEMA's national geospatial infrastructure, used by millions annually.

2021

FEMA · NiyamIT · National Flood Insurance Program

Risk Rating Engine 2.0 — Modernizing the Science Behind Flood Insurance Pricing

FEMA's legacy flood insurance rating system had used a simplistic zone-based model for decades — if your property was in a flood zone, you paid a fixed rate based on that zone designation, regardless of your actual, individualized risk. Risk Rating 2.0 replaced that with a sophisticated actuarial pricing engine incorporating multiple property-level variables: first-floor height, distance to the nearest water feature, foundation type, structure characteristics, and multiple flood types (riverine, coastal, storm surge, heavy rainfall).

As GIS Director at NiyamIT, I led the development and transition effort — coordinating across FEMA's technology, actuarial, policy, and communications teams. This required deep technical understanding of how spatial data from the NFHL integrated with actuarial models, as well as extensive stakeholder management with state and local floodplain managers who depended on the rating system being accurate, explainable, and defensible.

Why it mattered: Risk Rating 2.0 launched October 2021 and represented the most significant transformation of NFIP pricing in the program's 50+ year history. It introduced genuine, science-based individualized risk pricing for approximately 5 million NFIP policyholders — making insurance costs more equitable and encouraging risk-informed decisions about where and how people build.

2018

FEMA · IBM · Risk Analysis & Management

Risk Analysis & Management (RAM) System — Spatial Decision Intelligence for Flood Managers

The RAM System is one of FEMA's core spatial decision-support platforms, enabling federal and state flood risk managers to query, analyze, and visualize the full body of national flood mapping data. Leading the RAM delivery program at IBM, I managed a 100+ person cross-functional organization through a complete security authorization process to achieve Authority to Operate (ATO), while simultaneously delivering 20+ system enhancements in a single year.

My technical contributions went beyond program management: I served as the domain translator between FEMA's hydrological and actuarial scientists, the IT architects, and the policy stakeholders — a role that required genuine fluency in how spatial flood data is generated, validated, and consumed by decision-makers at every level of government.

Why it mattered: RAM is the analytical backbone for FEMA's Risk MAP program — the national effort to produce and maintain scientifically accurate flood maps. Improvements to RAM directly improve the quality and accessibility of flood risk intelligence for state floodplain managers, engineers, and emergency planners.
Spatial Decision Support ATO / Security Authorization Risk MAP Flood Data Analytics Program Delivery

2014

FEMA · IBM · Risk MAP

Risk MAP Spatial Workflow Automation — Replacing Manual GIS Processes at Scale

FEMA's Risk MAP program produces and maintains flood hazard maps for communities across the United States. In the early phase of my IBM career, I identified critical bottlenecks in how spatial data was being validated, transformed, and delivered — processes that required significant manual GIS analyst effort for each flood mapping project.

I engineered automated spatial data pipelines using Python and JavaScript that replaced these manual workflows — integrating GIS visualization features, data validation routines, and transformation logic into repeatable, auditable automated processes. I also collaborated across FEMA and private-sector mapping partners to define standardized data management practices, producing technical governance manuals that became operational standards for the program.

Why it mattered: Automation at this scale meant faster map production cycles, fewer errors, and significantly reduced costs for the Risk MAP program — ultimately accelerating the delivery of accurate flood hazard data to communities that use it to make life-safety and land-use decisions.
